Here, you can see all the detectors that we deployed with a more easily grasped view. Monitoring of deployed guardrails are crucial due to a number of factors. 

. We can detect misuse and policy violations. We may find out how often rules are triggered and which ones. 
. We get operational insights, and spikes in guardrail detections may indicate someone trying to bypass the guardrails. 
. We get logs and metrics that can be a part of compliance reporting. Proving that Peach Happens Produce are actively blocking and monitoring attempts.
. We can use them to optimise user experience. Too many false positive (blocking when it was okay) can frustrate the users and with monitoring we can finetune the guardrails over time.

However, if you looked closely at the dashboard, you might have found a fifth metric that currently has no values. It belongs to the first defence layer we put up. The system prompt. 


We have external AI models blocking, but, the LLM is also able to deny requests. If a prompt didnt trigger any of the guardrails on the way in, they may still get denied by the system prompt. 
This is an important metric for us, because otherwise it may look like we are approving more requests than we actually are.

== The Reality of having LLMs as judges

There are many advantages to using LLMs to judge input and output response. 
They have a stronger semantic understanding and can understand context, intent and paraphrashing better than rules, keyword filters and classifier models. 

They are also very flexible and adaptable since they are prompted, just like we did PeachPal, to look for specific things. 
Updating topics is very easy, and there is often no need to find a specialised model to deploy or retrain it. 

Some downsides are, of course, cost and latency. LLMs are in general larger and more compute intensive than smaller classifier models. 
Currently, there is a lot of research and development using smaller LLMs or quantized version to lower the compute footprint.
There is also a change to bypass LLM-judges via prompt injection or attacks that attempt to influence judges reasoining or instructions.

LLM-decision may also be harder to trace and explain, especially in compliance-sensitive environments.
